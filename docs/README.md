# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

## Overview
OctoAcme uses a structured project lifecycle to move from idea to delivery with clear ownership, iterative execution, and measurable outcomes. The approach begins with project initiation to validate the business need, align stakeholders, and define a lightweight plan. It then moves into planning, where the team translates approved initiatives into backlog items, priorities, milestones, risks, and release expectations. Throughout execution, project teams stay aligned through daily standups, weekly syncs, pull request review standards, and defined quality gates. The process closes with release verification, stakeholder communication, and retrospectives that turn lessons learned into actionable improvements.

## Core Principles
- Customer-first: prioritize customer value and usability.
- Iterative delivery: deliver small, testable increments.
- Clear ownership: each project has a named Project Manager and Product Lead.
- Data-informed decisions: measure impact and iterate based on evidence.
- Psychological safety: encourage feedback, transparency, and learning.

## Summary of OctoAcme Project Management Processes
OctoAcme’s project management model is built around a lightweight yet disciplined lifecycle. It begins with initiation to confirm business value and align stakeholders, then moves into planning to define scope, ownership, milestones, and delivery expectations. As work begins, the team manages execution through a visible project board, clear pull request standards, and regular communication rhythms such as standups and weekly status syncs. The process is designed to identify risks early, ensure transparency across functions, and keep delivery focused on customer and business outcomes.

The model also relies on clearly defined roles and responsibilities. Product leaders define the desired outcome and success metrics, project managers coordinate timelines and dependencies, developers build and validate solutions, and QA/testing functions ensure quality and acceptance criteria are met. Communication is intentionally structured through stakeholder updates, weekly reviews, and escalation paths for blockers or incidents, helping teams stay aligned without losing momentum. Quality assurance is embedded throughout the workflow through testing, CI checks, release gating, smoke tests, and post-deployment verification, reinforcing a consistent standard for delivering reliable change.
